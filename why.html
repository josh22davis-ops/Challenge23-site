A Different Kind of Fitness App

I stopped tracking my workouts — and started tracking my fitness.

Logging sets and reps felt like homework with no payoff. I'd open the app, type in what I did, and close it. Weeks later I had a spreadsheet of numbers but no idea if I was actually getting fitter.

Most fitness apps are built around logging. They track what you did — not what it did to you. More reps, more weight, more data. But none of it tells you whether your cardiovascular system is getting stronger.

Now I get a single performance score after every 23-minute session. No manual input. No guessing.

Challenge 23 runs the same structured workout every time — a 23-minute session with a warmup, five interval blocks, a final work segment, and a 2-minute cooldown that measures your 120-second heart rate drop. Your heart rate is tracked the entire time, mapped against every phase. At the end, you get a Training Quality score that tells you exactly how you performed.

Heart rate recovery is one of the strongest indicators of cardiovascular health. Doctors use it. Researchers publish on it. But no fitness app actually tracks it — you're left checking your own pulse with no record.

Challenge 23 measures it automatically. After every work phase, the app records how far your heart rate drops in 60 seconds. Six times per session. No manual pulse checks. No guessing.

Because the workout never changes, I can actually see my fitness improving week over week.

That's the thing most people miss. If you do a different workout every day, there's no baseline. You can't compare Tuesday's circuit to Thursday's HIIT class. The variables are different every time.

Challenge 23 locks the structure so your data becomes meaningful. Your heart rate during block 3 this week can be directly compared to block 3 last week. Your recovery speed after the second interval can be tracked over months. The workout and the measurement are the same thing.
